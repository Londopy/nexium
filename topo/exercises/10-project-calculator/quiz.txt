Q: In a recursive-descent parser, why does `term` call `power` rather than the other way round?
- `power` is the shorter function
* each level handles only its own operators and hands tighter-binding ones down, so `*` binds tighter than `+` and `^` tighter than `*`
- the order does not matter
> Precedence is the order of the calls: the deeper the level, the tighter its operators bind.

Q: What does this print? Subtraction is handled in a loop, left to right.
| fn main() {
|     var v: i64 = 10
|     for x in [3, 2, 1] { v = v - x }
|     println("{} {}", .{v, 10 - (3 - (2 - 1))})
| }
ask: output
* 4 8
- 4 4
- 8 8
> A loop groups from the left, as `expression` does; nesting to the right is what `power` does for `^`.

Q: `s.vars.get(n) orelse return error.UnknownName`: what happens when the name is missing?
- the program panics
* the whole call returns the error `UnknownName`
- `get` returns zero
> `orelse` runs its right side when the optional is `null`, and that right side leaves the function with the error.
